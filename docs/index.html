How predictions are made

Each team's form is its most recent results in all competitions (friendlies excluded), using windows of 5, 6, 7 and 8 matches. A team needs at least 7 recorded matches before it is predicted. Every rule below adds weight to home win (1), draw (X) or away win (2); the highest total is the pick.

  • Balance (pp. 46–48): the result that makes the two 6-match forms, plus this match, as equal as possible.
  • Common zero (p. 47): if neither team has drawn in 6, the draw fills the gap.
  • Trailing team (pp. 79–81): the side with fewer positive results (wins + draws) over 7 does not lose; stronger when the 5-match form confirms it and at home. This is the book's main bet, shown as “Underdog”.
  • Unbeaten 7 vs one loss (p. 81): a side unbeaten in 7 facing a side with exactly one loss in 7 tends to lose.
  • Six wins in a row (p. 68): struggles to win again. Six defeats (p. 69): hard to beat by a moderate opponent.
  • Special forms: home side at 1-1-4 doesn't lose to weaker form (p. 67); 4-1-1 gives up points only to equal or better form (p. 68); 1-2-3 is ready for a 4th loss, 2-1-3 is very hard to beat (p. 70).
  • Back home (p. 70): three straight losses, then a home game → bounce back.
  • Golden sequence (pp. 59–60): results that complete a 4-2-1 form over 7 get a small boost.

These rules are an interpretation of the book's text; the book does not give exact weights. Results are judged on the 90-minute score. Predictions are computed only from matches played before kick-off, so the record cannot see the future. This is a research tool, not betting advice.
